    For parents and teachers

    A guide that builds thinking, not copying

    Breaking the Jargons is a self-study space for children in Class 1 to 10 across India. Every subject becomes an interactive world, and every child gets a path towards the career they dream of, whether or not there's a good school nearby.

    For every child

    Made for children in villages and small towns as much as big cities. It works in a phone's web browser, with no sign-up and no app to install.

    Dreams become plans

    A child who wants to be a doctor or an engineer sees the steps to get there, and the skills each step builds.

    Thinking first

    Pip asks for a prediction before explaining, gives hints when a child is stuck, and never hands over homework answers.

    Right for their class

    The class setting changes Pip's words and depth, so a Class 2 child and a Class 9 child each get explanations they can use.

    Their own language

    English lessons for children learning it for the first time, and explanations in Indian languages as they're added.

    Safe by design

    Short answers, no personal information collected, stays on the subject, and encourages children to share what they find with you.

    Built with Claude

    Pip runs on Claude, by Anthropic

    Pip is powered by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. Claude doesn't only chat. Inside each world it uses tools to act, so children see the answer happen instead of just reading it.

    • Understands questions in a child's own words, including half-formed ones and mixed languages.
    • Teaches by asking. Claude is instructed to ask for a guess, give hints and praise effort.
    • Acts in the world with tools: fly the plane to Antarctica, change an experiment, replay the step where code went wrong.
    • Adjusts to each class, from first words in Class 1 to real terms in Class 10.
    • Builds each child's path, linking every new puzzle to the dream they picked.
    • Explains in Indian languages, and helps children learn English through the language they already know.

    The Claude key stays on our server, never in the browser. Pip is in preview while live Claude answers are switched on, world by world.
